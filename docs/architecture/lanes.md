# Account lanes

A driver that reads an account's quota windows reports them per step through `ProviderReport.quota` (domain/driver.ts, `reportProviderQuota`), and the loop notes them on the account the step's request went out on (`CredentialPlacement.noteQuota`). Quota state belongs to a sign-in, not to its label: each report, reading and segment carries the stamp of the credential it was for (`AuthInfo.signedInAt`), each lane's accounts carry the stamp the store holds now (`credentialLane`, through `ModelRegistry.stamp`), and placement binds each label to that sign-in. A new sign-in under a label starts with no reading, and a late report of a sign-in its label replaced since is dropped. A rename in /auth (`auth.renameKey`) tells the placement (`renameLabel`): the readings and holds go with the sign-in to its new label, and a late report under the old label counts on the new one. A stamp names a sign-in only under its own label: two labels whose sign-ins share a stamp stay two accounts. The Anthropic driver reads them on a Claude plan sign-in, from every answer, a 429 included (`unifiedQuota`, anthropic.ts): `anthropic-ratelimit-unified-{5h,7d}-utilization` (the fraction used) and `-reset` (epoch seconds), and the account's `-status` and `-representative-claim`; the names are the ones Claude Code 2.1.306 reads, and an answer with no status says nothing. A refused account (`rejected`, its claim) holds that window, full when no utilization is given, until its reset, and the placement holds the account while that reading stands: the hold is read from the readings, so a server that loads them after a restart holds it too. The OpenAI driver reads them on a ChatGPT plan sign-in, from every Codex answer, a 429 included (`codexQuota`, openai.ts): `x-codex-{primary,secondary}-used-percent` (0 to 100), `-window-minutes` (the window's name, `5h` or `7d`, comes from it) and `-reset-at` (epoch seconds), the names Codex reads (openai/codex `342ed69`); a window at 100% is rejected until its reset, as credits past it are extra usage and never quota, and other limit families (`x-<limit>-…`, single models) are not read. An API key gets none. A driver may instead limit a model by what it costs (`ModelDriverContribution.spendLimits`: at most `usd` of list price in any trailing `window`). The OpenCode Go driver sets 20%, 50% and 100% of each model's monthly dollars over `5h`, `7d` and `31d` (`goSpendLimits`, opencode.ts; `OPENCODE_GO_PLAN=plus` reads Go Plus, any other value Go), and none on a free model or on Zen. Gent meters each account's own priced spend on such a model per hour (`noteFuel` with `spendLimitsOf`, a step's and a summary's alike) and reads each limit as that model's window (`meterReadings`): the spend in the trailing window over the limit, rejected at the limit (spend past it is extra usage, never quota) until the oldest hour whose leaving brings it under leaves, and its known spend a floor while an unpriced request falls in it: such a window stays rejected once its known spend reached the limit, and is unread otherwise. A request is priced at what its driver names for it (`requestPricing`, through `requestCost`, provider.ts; a step's, a summary's and `models.decide`'s cost alike), else at the model's list price: the Go driver bills a request past its model's long-context line at that tier's price, and a DeepSeek one double in the plan's peak hours (01:00 to 04:00 and 06:00 to 10:00 UTC, Monday to Friday; `goRequestPricing`). A metered window's fuel per percent is its limit's own (`meterRate`), and under its limit it carries one run at least. A new sign-in under the label starts with no metered spend, and a meter goes with its sign-in on a rename. A server that loads the ledger binds each label to the sign-in of its newest stamped reading or meter, so a meter keeps its sign-in after a restart, and a new sign-in after one still starts with none. A reading may name one model (`QuotaReading.model`): a plan that meters a model apart from the account reports that model's window beside the account's of the same name, and the readings, the segments and the ledger key it apart (`windowKey`). A rejected model window holds the account for that model's runs alone (`meters`, through `isHeld`); its segments count that model's fuel alone (`noteFuel` names the step's resolved model, a summary its own); a start admits against the account's windows and those of the model its route spends on (`LaneRequest.model`; a virtual model's default choice, `routeSpendModel`, turn.ts). `accounts.lanes` lists every window, a model's with its `model`, and counts the account's alone in an account's `sustainable` and the provider's `capacity`; the pool's weekly and five-hour pace reads the account's `7d` and `5h` alone, and a model's window is one of the other window ceilings when a start of that model is admitted. The TUI's binding window still reads every window, a model's among them. Every step and handoff summary on a credential is fuel on that account, pool or not (`noteFuel`): its list price over $5 per million tokens, so one fuel is one input token at that price, with its token classes kept; a price that is unknown, or $0 for tokens spent, is unknown fuel, never zero. Per account window, a segment runs from one reading to the first that moved at least 1% from where it opened, and closes with the fuel spent in it; a reset (its time moved by more than 60 s, or the window fell), a gap over 15 minutes, a refusal or unknown fuel drops it. Fuel per percent is `Σ fuel / Σ points` over the kept segments, each halved per 24 active hours after it; a segment that spent none, or whose move passes its fuel by more than 3 points at the rate of the other kept segments, each fuel at the model weights, is spend outside gent and left out (`outsideShare`); each segment is judged with itself left out, until the kept set holds still; a segment with no other kept segment is kept. `band` is `±0.5 / Σ points` kept. A window with no rate of its own reads the provider's over every account. A plan does not bill each model at its list price, so a segment keeps its fuel by model (`FuelSegment.models`) and its edge steps' fuel (`edges`); `fitModelWeights` fits a quota weight per model over the account windows' kept segments: least squares of each move at its window's weighted rate against `Σ weight × fuel`, each row over its edges' rounding variance and its account's window count, a one-point prior at 1, fitted over a 24-hour span with a standard error under 10%, else 1, clamped to [1/8, 8]; windows and weights refit until still (`fitProviderWindows`). `accounts.lanes` reports them (`models`), and each account window its models' fuel per percent (`LaneWindow.models`). Admission still counts runs alike. A window carries `(1 − u) × 100 × fuelPerPct / (fuel per lane-hour × hours to its reset)` runs (a reset that passed reads full, over the window's length); an account the fewest of its windows (0 while held). These per-account rates still decide under-pace placement and reset-first ranking; they do not divide the pool's rolling forecast among accounts. A provider's `capacity` paces its scope (the pool, else the order's first credential) in fuel/hour. Its weekly floor is the largest cumulative in-hand quota divided by time to each reset deadline. Its rolling rate is the in-hand weekly quota plus each fresh week's share inside the next 168 hours (`full × max(0, 168 − hoursToReset) / 168`), divided by 168 hours. The target is `max(floor, rolling)`, capped by weekly and five-hour ceilings. A weekly prefix ceiling divides all fuel available before each reset by elapsed hours, then credits that refill; simultaneous resets share the same pre-refill bound. An account's 5h window counts only to 90% of its fuel, in the window's own pace and in the five-hour ceiling (`FIVE_HOUR_SHARE`, provider.ts), so a burst of running work stops short of the hard limit; a weekly window and a model's own window count to their end. A window at its share carries no run before any rate is read, an account with such a window carries none whatever its other windows' unknown paces, a pool whose every account carries none admits no start, and placement puts an account with no room after every account with some. A turn already running keeps its account until the provider refuses it. The five-hour ceiling includes only accounts with weekly room now, plus a spent week's fresh five-hour room from its reset when that falls within five hours; a reset in six hours adds none. An independently rejected five-hour window still holds until its own reset. Short-window fuel is bounded by weekly room on the same account, so complementary windows cannot create fuel. Every refill is credited after its prefix bound, and refilled short-window fuel must also last five hours. Gent uses a 100% quota target. Every hour counts alike: the floor and the rolling supply spread each window's fuel evenly over the hours to its reset, so the pool spends at a steady rate until its quotas are used up. The owner's day and night pacing (a `night` setting that weighted night hours and capped a project's night lanes) was removed on 2026-10-26; a user file that still sets `night` loads, the key is ignored and a config write keeps it as written. `capacity = floor(min(target, weekly ceiling, five-hour ceiling, other window ceilings) / fuelPerHour)`, with one final floor. Unknown readings or rates keep capacity unknown; an entirely held scope is zero. Explicit credential refusals contribute no forecast; quota rejections contribute their future refill but stay held for present placement. A scope without weekly readings keeps the sum of account minima. Pools with no readings keep stage-0 ranking. A pinned scope cannot borrow another account's refill. Fuel per lane-hour is measured: a lane that ends adds its fuel and its hours to its provider's burn, the burn before it halved per 24 lane-hours, and a lane with unknown fuel, or of a provider gent read no window of, adds nothing; until the provider saw 1 lane-hour it is the prior, 3.4 M. A lane of a model gent reads a window of adds to that model's burn too (`modelBurns`), and a model's window paces its runs at that model's rate (`modelFuelPerHourOf`): its measured burn once its lanes saw 1 lane-hour, else the prior times the model's price over the reference model's (`priceRatioOf`: its metered fuel over the same tokens at the reference weights, input 1, output 5, cache read 0.1, cache write 1.25), else the provider's. `accounts.lanes` reports it (`fuelPerHour`) with the lane-hours behind it (`laneHours`). `accounts.lanes` (rpc.ts, no session: the accounts are the server's, the scope the launch profile's config) answers each provider gent read a window of, with its `load`. A run's lane is the route its session's turns take (`credentialLane`: the pin, else the pool, else the order's first credential). `CredentialPlacement.admitLane` admits a run that another run starts (threads, through `ExtensionContext.Accounts.admit`), through a fair queue per pool (provider and scope, `laneQueueKey`): the load is the running turns and the admitted starts that share an account with the scope, the asker's own turn left out; the account's windows count each run of that load at its model's share of a mean run (`runShare`: the model's fuel per hour at its quota weight over the provider's, the weights scaled so their fitted mix weighs 1), so a cheap model's run holds part of a lane and the report's `load` is a fraction; a model's own windows count only its runs and starts of that model, whole (each keeps the model its route spends on), and size the account's windows in runs of that model; a start whose share does not fit beside the load under the capacity of either waits in the queue (`LaneAdmission.Queued`, with the waiters ahead of it), and one whose share does not fit even with nothing running is refused for good with `LaneUnavailable` (its `retryAt` the soonest reset or hold end, at least 60 s on), and an unknown capacity admits. The queue (`serve`, provider.ts) orders its waiters by the lanes their asking session holds on the provider (open turns and grants of the starts it asked for), fewest first, then by arrival, then by key; a waiter that fits takes a lane (`Admitted` when it got one in its own admission's serve), and the walk starts again. Each serve first drops what time ended (`trim`: an account hold or a reading past its end), and reads each waiter's accounts under the sign-ins their labels hold now (`currentScope`), never under the stamps it asked with: a newer sign-in reported while it waited is what it is served on, and its old stamp never rebinds the label. Strict head of line: behind a waiter the account's windows cannot carry, no later waiter takes a pool lane, so a large run is not passed by small ones; a waiter that waits only for its model's own windows blocks nobody. A waiter whose windows become spent leaves for good with the refusal (`awaitLane` fails). A lane end (`release`), a grant that joins its session's open turn (`openLane`), a freed or lapsed grant, a waiter that leaves (`freeReservation`) and a quota reading (`noteQuota`) each serve the queue; while runs wait, one alarm serves it again at the next window reset or account hold end still ahead after that trim, the change no run's end reports. No timer polls. A waiter keeps its place when admitted again under its key on the same route, and gives it up on another; `awaitLane` waits for its grant and takes it: a taken grant no longer lapses, and holds its lane until its turn opens or a free names it, so a start's send is never raced by its own lapse. Spent stays a refusal, not a wait: its wait runs to a window's reset, up to a week, and the queue is in memory, so a machine waits it out durably on its run's record (`Waiting`, `lane`). The queue lives in the placement's memory: a restart starts it empty, and each queued thread round asks again when its starter's loop opens (threads.md). The check reads the capacity unfloored, so a window short of a whole lane still carries a run whose share fits; the report and `LaneUnavailable.capacity` floor it to whole lanes, and the TUI row shows a free count under a tenth as `<0.1` (`laneRows`, apps/tui/src/utils.ts). The pace alone admits a run, and the fair queue orders who goes next: no project holds lanes. The owner's project lane holds (`providers.<owner>.authHold`, which admitted a project's runs up to its hold whatever the pace and kept other work off the held lanes not in use, with a `hold` row per project in the band) were removed on 2026-10-26, as the owner wants a steady flow until the quotas are used up; a user file that still sets `authHold` loads and the key is ignored; a config write keeps it while its provider entry keeps another field, and a write that empties the entry drops it with the entry, since nothing reads it. A project file that sets `extraUsage` is read without it and logs one warning for each file and field set. An admitted or granted start holds its lane under its message id until its turn opens (`openLane`, at each turn's start, a resumed turn included, which also takes a waiting start out of the queue), until its caller frees it (`ExtensionContext.Accounts.cancel`, `freeReservation`: a start that opens no turn of its own, as a round that joins its thread's running turn, one the thread refuses as busy, or one whose copy fails), or, until its caller takes it (`awaitLane`), for 2 minutes as a backstop, when the grant lapses and serves the queue on its own clock (a start that holds it through its preparation, `Accounts.hold`, keeps it while any hold's scope is open, and its 2 minutes start again when the last hold on that admission ends); the lane frees at the turn's end, before its `turnAfter` hooks, and on the turn's exit as a backstop. Only starts wait or are refused: a user's own turn is counted, never queued. The ledger also keeps the latest readings and the segments, 7 days and 500 per window at most, the burn, the model burns, and the meters until their hours leave the longest window, as optional `readings`, `segments`, `burn`, `modelBurn` and `meters` keys, written whenever there are any: an older file decodes with none, and an older binary ignores them. A value the window drops changes the ledger, so the next write leaves it off the file. A SIGKILL loses what changed since the last write (each hour as it closes, and shutdown); the running turns are in memory alone, so a server after a restart counts no lane until a cut turn runs again. An account whose rejected reading meters the step's model is filled for it until the last such reset (`CredentialPlacement.filledUntil`), read for the sign-in the store holds now: one the label replaced since has filled nothing. A step on a filled account leaves it before its request (`meetFullWindow`, turn.ts) for the next account of its order that is not filled, with a notice, and a spread run records the move `filled`. With none left, the turn goes on there only when the user file sets `providers.<id>.extraUsage` (off by default; a project file never sets it), with one notice a turn; else the step stops before its request, naming the first reset (`QuotaWindowsFull`, its `retryAt`). A handoff summary's spend may fill its account: the step after it meets the full window the same way. A moved step's cache is cold on the new account, so its window hands off the history before the turn's prompt when the resend costs more than the handoff (`accountMoved`, `coldHandoffPays`); a small history goes whole. A move back to the account the branch's last request ran on reads that warm cache. A lost login is a sign-in the provider no longer takes: the driver proved its credential absent or its refresh refused (`credentialFailure` `Unavailable` or `Rejected`), or the provider answered 401 to it (`AuthenticationError` `InvalidKey` or `ExpiredKey` with a response, after `authorizedClient` refreshed and sent it once more; `loginLoss` in runtime/provider.ts). A model the account does not serve, a quota, a rate limit, a 403, a missing key and a failure with no response are not losses. The turn records it on the slot its request used (`CredentialPlacement.loseLogin`, from `moveCredential`), pinned run or not, and logs `credential.login-lost` once per loss. The placement holds it in memory, keyed by the sign-in's stamp: a step served on the slot (`noteServed`, from the step's credential walk, neither cancelled nor cut) clears it, a new sign-in on the label (`forget`) clears it, a rename carries it, and a loss reported for an older stamp marks nothing. Quota headers and fuel do not clear it: a refusal carries them too, so a second refusal is no second loss. A summary does not clear it either: a compactor can succeed with no model request. A classifier call or a cache warm that a sign-in refuses records nothing; the next step on that sign-in refuses and records it. Claude Code's own account is `Rejected` when the token endpoint refused every stored refresh token and the CLI fallback failed too, as a linked directory's is. It holds no lane. `accounts.lanes` reports it as `LaneAccount.loginLost` (`since`, `reason`) and lists a provider by its loss alone; the band and `gent ctl lanes --line` print `login! <provider> <slot> <refused|unavailable> · sign in with /auth` after the provider's row, before its holds. A restarted server forgets it until the next refused request.

The `@gent/login-alert` extension tells the owner away from the band. After each turn it runs the environment's `GENT_LOGIN_ALERT_COMMAND` with `sh -c`, once for each loss `accounts.lanes` lists that it has not alerted yet, with the band's line in `GENT_LOGIN_ALERT` and a 10 s bound. Each command runs on the resource's scope, so a turn's end never waits for one. Only the environment gent starts in sets the command; a project config cannot. With no command it reads no lanes. The alerted losses are server memory keyed by provider, slot and `since`; a loss the report no longer lists is forgotten, so the next loss on that sign-in alerts again. A rename moves a loss to a new slot and a changed extension build starts with no alerted losses, so either can alert one loss twice; a loss a served step clears before the turn ends alerts nothing, as the band no longer lists it. A failed command logs `login-alert.command.failed`. A macOS notification: `GENT_LOGIN_ALERT_COMMAND='osascript -e "display notification (system attribute \"GENT_LOGIN_ALERT\") with title \"gent\""'`.
